      Result & Inference
      
Every extra row multiplies the number of column choices that have to be checked, so the search space grows factorially with board size. Backtracking keeps this workable by abandoning a row's placement the moment a conflict is detected, instead of building out full boards that were doomed from the first bad move — which is why the solve times for n=4, 6, and 8 diverge so quickly even though the rule being checked at each cell is identical.

With 5 cities, fixing the start leaves 4! = 24 orderings to check, which is still small enough to brute-force in an instant. That factor grows so fast that a 15-city version of the same approach would already be computationally out of reach — which is the practical motivation for reduction-and-bound techniques that discard whole branches of unfinished tours instead of completing every one of them.
